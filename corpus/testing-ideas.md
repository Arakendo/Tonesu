## Translation targets not yet attempted

- [x] **Multi-agent institutional crisis narrative — "The Archive Collapse"** — filed as ACC-001 through ACC-006 (S1116–S1156). Confirmed that Tonesu handles institutional fracture, legitimacy drift, bureaucracy, coercive substitution, and partial repair well when the ontology remains structural. Key finding: the language is strongest when the scene can stay anchored to visible role chains, support-dependencies, and causal topology rather than unstable private affect alone.

	**Core scenario:** a regional archive system discovers altered records; some witnesses lie honestly; some institutions act legally but illegitimately; violence prevents wider collapse; truth becomes only partially knowable; public trust decays unevenly; coordination survives temporarily; emotional pressure rises; different groups produce incompatible but internally coherent interpretations.

	**Why this batch matters:** it pressures almost every claim the language makes about itself at once — compositional reuse, ambiguity boundaries, ontology discipline, causality, emergence/decay, emotional expression, institutional language, recursive attribution, and overload drift.

	**Required domains:** epistemics · legitimacy · causality · emergence/decay · institutions · emotion · negotiation · archive/memory · violence · time · counterfactuals · responsibility.

	**Required phase structure:**
	1. **Stable order** — archive functioning, hearings coherent, witnesses agreeing, authority trusted.
	2. **Local contradiction** — one inconsistency appears; must remain ambiguous between error, fraud, uncertainty, and interpretation gap.
	3. **Distributed interpretation split** — multiple institutions produce incompatible readings in good faith.
	4. **Violence emerges** — force used "temporarily" to preserve order; test `ka-ra` / `o-ra` / `su-ra` asymmetry.
	5. **Emotional/social strain** — panic, grief, distrust, mechanical obedience, public loyalty-performance.
	6. **Partial recovery** — no full resolution; some truths recover, some do not, some institutions survive only procedurally.

	**Hard requirements:**
	- honest disagreement, not just deception
	- technically true but structurally misleading statements
	- delayed causality, where small semantic errors become institutional failures
	- emotional non-propositional moments (silence, refusal, broken speech, panic speech, ritual repetition)
	- institutional disagreement without reducing everything to villainy

	**Key lexical/structural pressure points already identified:** `si` / `to` / `no-si` / `to-fe-ka` / `to-fe-ki` in contradiction handling; legitimacy vs coercion (`su-ra`, `wi-fe`, `wi-o-li`, `ka-ra`); willing vs imposed action (`wi-ka`, `su-ka`); decay/emergence chains (`ki lo-de`, `be`, `ki lo no-pa`); archive-trust and publication-state questions around recorded truth.

	**Experimental evaluation protocol:** after writing the same corpus, give it independently to multiple humans and multiple AIs; ask each to reconstruct (1) what happened, (2) who was legitimate, (3) what caused collapse, (4) who acted lawfully, (5) who acted correctly, (6) where violence emerged, and (7) what became unknowable. Compare semantic recovery, not just sentence-by-sentence translation.

	**Failure signals:** constant glossing required; primitive meanings drift per scene; causal topology cannot be recovered; emotional scenes become robotic; institutional distinctions collapse together; all ambiguity requires meta commentary.

	**Success signals:** independent readers recover similar event structure; unstated relations are inferred correctly; compositions remain reusable across phases; new compounds emerge naturally; causal asymmetries survive compression; readers begin temporarily reasoning inside the system rather than translating out of it.

	**Design note:** this batch likely depends on EM-001 (performative / expressive register) being at least partially resolved or consciously bracketed as an open failure surface. Treat that as a feature of the test, not a reason to avoid it.

- [x] **Emotionally unstable family-reunion narrative — "The Returning Brother"** — filed as RBR-001 through RBR-005 (S1157–S1186). Confirmed that Tonesu can sustain unstable private relation better once the scene stays anchored to bodily thresholds, kinship claims, silence, memory, and explicit non-forgiveness rather than abstract emotional explanation alone. The deliberate opposite of Archive Collapse. Strip away institutions, legitimacy, and public procedure; test whether Tonesu can preserve unstable human feeling without flattening it into abstract declarations. The target is emotionally pressurized interpersonal reality: ambiguity, shame, affection, resentment, silence, grief, partial forgiveness, and incompatible memories surviving compositional compression.

	**Core scenario:** two siblings reunite after many years. The older sibling left during crisis, justified leaving rationally, sent aid occasionally, but never returned. The younger sibling stayed, cared for the dying parent, absorbed the material and emotional cost, and both resents and misses the older sibling. The parent has recently died. Now the older sibling returns.

	**Why this batch matters:** ACC showed that Tonesu can dominate structural legitimacy topology. This test asks the opposite question: whether human emotional instability can survive compositional compression without flattening, abstraction drift, philosophy-laundering, or emotional deadness. If Tonesu survives this, then `fe` and related relational structure are actually integrated rather than merely elegant in institutional scenes.

	**Required domains:** kinship · memory · grief · shame · affection · resentment · silence · avoidance · partial forgiveness · embodiment · indirect speech · emotional uncertainty.

	**Required emotional structures:**
	- simultaneous love and resentment
	- guilt without direct confession
	- gratitude mixed with anger
	- avoidance as meaningful non-action
	- emotional silence as signal rather than empty absence
	- performative normalcy masking unstable affect
	- partial forgiveness without completed reconciliation
	- grief displaced onto objects, habits, or side-arguments
	- emotional uncertainty in self-report and introspection
	- asymmetric memory of the same shared past
	- mismatched relational goals (one wants reconciliation; one wants recognition of pain)

	**Required phase structure:**
	1. **Arrival** — awkward politeness, indirectness, surface management, silence pressure.
	2. **Shared memory drift** — same events described differently; not factual contradiction but affective divergence.
	3. **Buried resentment leakage** — hesitation, loaded phrasing, avoided names, interruptions, displaced anger.
	4. **Grief rupture** — the dead parent becomes active through memory, accusation, regret, and longing; one sibling breaks, the other cannot answer correctly.
	5. **Failed resolution** — no clean reconciliation; partial understanding, lingering resentment, continued affection, unresolved loss.

	**Hard requirements:**
	- do not let explicit emotion labels dominate the batch
	- include emotionally false or unstable statements (for example, welcome without emotional simplicity)
	- include unfinished sentences, interruptions, and suspended propositions
	- include embodied details: handling objects, doorway attention, not eating, held breath, eye-avoidance, posture shifts
	- preserve emotional asymmetry rather than collapsing both siblings into one shared state
	- test whether readers can infer emotion without explanatory glossing

	**Key lexical/structural pressure points already identified:** performative fragments and silence (`—`, EM-001 surface pressure); memory vs knowledge (`to-ko`, `to-ko-re`, `no-to`); kinship vocabulary and address; contradiction without public adjudication; bodily signal and avoidance behavior; emotionally false surface speech; unfinished clause structure under pressure.

	**Experimental evaluation protocol:** after drafting the same corpus, ask independent readers to reconstruct (1) who wanted what, (2) what each sibling remembers differently, (3) where affection survived, (4) where resentment leaked, (5) whether forgiveness occurred, and (6) which emotions were inferred rather than named. Compare affective recovery, not just factual paraphrase.

	**Failure signals:** everything becomes explicit feeling labels; characters sound like philosophical entities instead of people; emotion collapses into trust/support/legitimacy language; bodily immediacy disappears; every state becomes structurally tidy; scenes require meta commentary to feel alive.

	**Success signals:** readers infer unstated tension, guilt, grief, affection, and resentment; silence carries meaning; different readers recover compatible but not identical emotional maps; compositions feel pressurized rather than decorative; the language resists the urge to explain because implication is already doing the work.

	**Design note:** this batch should be treated as a direct pressure test on EM-001 rather than postponed until EM-001 is fully solved. The whole point is to discover whether Tonesu can carry emotionally unstable relational reality before its expressive machinery feels clean.

- [x] **Synthetic lyric-image sequence — "Moon on the River"** — filed as LYR-001 through LYR-005 (S1187–S1216). First findings: Tonesu can open a lyric field through juxtaposition, motion/stillness contrast, layered sound and silence, and suspended metaphor before any human narrative agent appears; LYR-002 shows that repeated `helm` pressure can support controlled metaphor drift without collapsing into literal ontology or prose explanation; LYR-003 shows that exact line recurrence and slight refrain-variation can carry new pressure by position alone; LYR-004 shows that a late-entering `tu` can create apostrophic pressure through absence and non-answer without forcing the poem into overt dialogue; LYR-005 shows that the track can close by transformed return-image and residue rather than thesis. This is the first extended poetry-specific stress track after the earlier single-poem probes (BSH-001, EMD-001).

	**Core scenario:** not a plot but a lyric image-field. Moon, river, rain, stone, stars, sound, and silence recur across a short sequence. The opening batches must feel like a poem beginning to think, not a narrator beginning to explain. Later batches pressure metaphor drift, refrain-variation, a late-entering lyric addressee, and closure by transformed return-image rather than explicit summary.

	**Why this batch matters:** Basho and Dickinson already proved that Tonesu can analyze isolated poetic pressure points. This test asks the harder question: can the language sustain poetry as an extended mode without collapsing into prose-with-line-breaks, hidden narrative reporting, or analytic over-clarification? The target is lyric continuity: image recurrence, suspended implication, and meaning that accumulates by return rather than by explicit argument.

	**Required domains:** moon / stars · river / rain / water-sound · stone / stillness · silence · metaphor · refrain / recurrence · lyric address · suspension.

	**Required poetic structures:**
	1. **Image sequencing without plot dependence** — the poem must move by recurrence and pressure between images, not by narrated events.
	2. **Juxtaposition without causal cleanup** — `/`, `:`, and simple clause pairing should remain capable of holding images together without forcing explanation.
	3. **Metaphor that stays poetic** — later batches should use `helm`, `helms`, or suspended near-equivalence only where the line remains image-legible and does not collapse into doctrinal identity.
	4. **Sound and silence co-presence** — the poem should support acoustic contradiction as a lyric fact rather than immediately resolving it propositionally.
	5. **Refrain with semantic drift** — a line or near-line should return later carrying changed pressure without new primitive support.
	6. **Late human pressure** — if a speaker or addressee enters, it should feel like lyric compression, not scene prose.

	**Required phase structure:**
	1. **Image field** — moon, river, rain, stone, stars, sound, silence; no explanation.
	2. **Metaphor drift** — one or two images begin to act on each other in physically false but poetically legible ways.
	3. **Refrain variation** — an earlier line returns with altered force.
	4. **Absent address** — a late `tu` or quoted call enters without converting the poem into dialogue.
	5. **Return image** — the opening image comes back changed; closure is by residue, not thesis.

	**Hard requirements:**
	- do not let the poem become a disguised narrative scene
	- do not solve every image relation with explicit causal or philosophical language
	- let at least one line remain physically false but emotionally legible
	- keep line-level language concrete before any abstract interpretation appears
	- test whether recurrence can carry meaning without new vocabulary
	- preserve room for ambiguity without making the poem structurally unreadable

	**Key lexical / structural pressure points already identified:** `—` as suspended poetic turn; `/` and `:` as image-linking choices; existential lightness (`pa`) vs over-predication; `no-so` and `ma-ki-so` in the same lyric field; `helm` for controlled metaphor pressure; celestial-body imagery (`nu-de-lu-mu`, `pu-lu-mu`) against material-motion imagery (`ma-di`, `no-ko'ma-ki`, `ma-su`).

	**Experimental evaluation protocol:** after drafting the same corpus, ask independent readers to report (1) what images recur, (2) where metaphor begins, (3) whether the poem feels narrative or lyric, (4) which relations are inferred rather than stated, and (5) whether the closing return-image feels earned. Compare poetic recovery, not just paraphrase.

	**Failure signals:** the poem reads like prose summary; every line demands glossing; metaphor collapses into ontology lecture; image-relations become over-explicit; recurrence feels decorative instead of pressurized; the track needs a narrator too early to stay legible.

	**Success signals:** readers recover a coherent image-field without a plot summary; repeated lines or images feel changed by position alone; suspension and juxtaposition carry real pressure; the language can hold poetic implication without abandoning structural clarity.

	**Design note:** treat this as a stress test on the dual register and on Tonesu's tolerance for controlled incompletion. Poetry should expose where the formal core is enough, where lyric work belongs to register practice, and where the language must simply accept a limit.

## Legal / statutory translation stress tests

**Philosophy**: Law relies on strategic vagueness. Tonesu exposes it structurally. Target text that is famous, short, and semantically messy. The goal is not to produce a "translation" but to show where the original text breaks down — places where scope is ambiguous, agent roles are underdefined, or normative / causal claims are blurred.

**Recommended sequence**: Section 230 → GDPR Art 6(1) → PATRIOT Act §215 → ACA mandate → (escalate to USA Freedom Act if you want suffering)

- [x] **CDA Section 230(c)(1)** — filed as CDA-001 (S639–S644). W200 (`su-ka-li`), W201 (`si-go-li`), W202 (`to-ki'ne-su`). "No provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or speaker of any information provided by another information content provider." Key finding: `wi-fe'ka-to-fe` is the `~`-suppression mechanism; `~`-grade categories (`~su-ka-li`, `~si-go-li`, `~ka-to-fe`) underlie the statute's strategic vagueness. Reworked with `~` structural mode comparisons after `~` spec was extended.
- [x] **GDPR Article 6(1)** — filed as GDPR-001 (S925–S931). Confirms that the article is best modeled as a permission battery over one repeated processing act, not a taxonomy of six data-kinds. Key split: contract (`ne-to-fe`) vs legal obligation (`wi-fe-su`) vs official authority (`wi-ra`). Key catch-all: `~vo-ne` for "legitimate interests."
- [ ] **USA PATRIOT Act Section 215 — "tangible things" collection** — Infamous surveillance clause. "Relevant to an authorized investigation" contains the entire controversy. Exposes: epistemic inflation ("necessary" vs "relevant"), scope ambiguity over what counts as tangible, hidden assumptions about data relevance. Run after Section 230 for the chaos escalation.
- [x] **ACA Individual Mandate — "minimum essential coverage"** — filed as ACA-001 (S645–S650). W203 (`wi-fe'de`), W204 (`su-zo-ko`), W205 (`ka-li-de`). The provision requiring coverage (26 U.S.C. §5000A). Famous for the "penalty vs tax" interpretive gymnastics. Exposes: category manipulation (exactly `to-fe-ka` territory), definitional ambiguity, causal vs normative claim confusion. Will produce at least one sentence that would be illegal in Tonesu.
- [ ] **Communications Decency Act Section 230(c)(2)** — The "Good Samaritan" good-faith moderation provision (the companion to 230(c)(1)). Tests conditional immunity with an explicit agent-volition component. Do after 230(c)(1).
- [ ] **California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) Section 1798.100** — Rights-based language: right to know, right to delete. More structured than GDPR. Exposes: what a "right" means structurally (obligation on the other party), scope of "personal information", obligations vs permissions. Good for contrast against GDPR after that batch.
- [ ] **USA Freedom Act Section 501** — Written to fix PATRIOT Act problems; layered amendments + dense cross-references. High risk of discovering new levels of structural suffering. Do last, as a boss fight.
