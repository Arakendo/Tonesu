## King Arthur — Round Table (S1024–S1028)

**Batch:** KAR-002  
**Source:** Arthurian Round Table material in the Malory/traditional register, focusing on shared counsel and anti-precedence rather than furniture description.  
**Entries:** S1024–S1028 · **New vocabulary:** none

**Key tests:**
- council as an institution rather than a decorative object
- `su-ne` for equal standing inside a political structure
- preserved kingship without solitary rule
- reciprocal speech between ruler and council
- rule constrained and shaped by counsel rather than dissolved into leaderlessness

**Central claim:** The Round Table matters because it changes the visible structure of rule. Arthur remains ruler, but the governing form no longer presents one elevated seat as the whole point of the room. Tonesu can make that explicit by treating the Round Table as a council-structure that creates equal standing among members while still leaving kingship intact.

---

### S1024 — KAR-002-A: Arthur establishes the Round Table as council

**Source:** "Arthur established the Round Table as a council."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Arthur  ka-be  lo-na RoundTable  /  lo-na RoundTable  ne  li-pu`

**Written:** `lana Arthur kabe lona RoundTable / lona RoundTable ne lipu`

**Gloss:** Arthur intentionally-creates RoundTable / RoundTable is council

**Natural reading:** Arthur establishes RoundTable, and RoundTable is a council.

**Notes:** The point is institutional. The Round Table is not mainly a piece of furniture; it is a structured council form.

---

### S1025 — KAR-002-B: Equal standing among members

**Source:** "The Round Table gave its members equal standing."

**Tonesu:** `la-na RoundTable  be  lo-li-pu-li  su-ne`

**Written:** `lana RoundTable be lolipuli sune`

**Gloss:** RoundTable establishes council-members equal-relation

**Natural reading:** RoundTable establishes equal standing among council-members.

**Notes:** `su-ne` is the real payoff. The legend's roundness matters because it encodes relational parity, not because circles are magically special.

---

### S1026 — KAR-002-C: Kingship remains, counsel equalizes relation

**Source:** "Arthur remained king, and the council stood in equality."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Arthur  ne  ra-su-li  /  la-li-pu  ne  su-ne`

**Written:** `lana Arthur ne rasuli / lalipu ne sune`

**Gloss:** Arthur is ruler / council is equal-relation

**Natural reading:** Arthur is ruler, and the council stands in equal relation.

**Notes:** This rejects a false choice. The Round Table does not erase kingship; it prevents kingship from exhausting the political form.

---

### S1027 — KAR-002-D: Shared deliberation

**Source:** "The council spoke to Arthur, and Arthur spoke to the council."

**Tonesu:** `la-li-pu  ka-si  lo-na Arthur  ;  la-na Arthur  ka-si  lo-li-pu`

**Written:** `lalipu kasi lona Arthur ; lana Arthur kasi lolipu`

**Gloss:** council speaks-to Arthur ; Arthur speaks-to council

**Natural reading:** The council speaks to Arthur, and Arthur speaks to the council.

**Notes:** Governance is dialogical here. The structure matters because counsel flows both directions instead of appearing as one-way command.

---

### S1028 — KAR-002-E: Counsel bounds rule

**Source:** "The Round Table did not abolish kingship; it bounded kingship within counsel."

**Tonesu:** `la-na RoundTable  no  de  lo-ra-su-li  /  ko  lo-ra-su-li  go-si  li-pu`

**Written:** `lana RoundTable no de lorasuli / ko lorasuli gosi lipu`

**Gloss:** RoundTable not decreases ruler / contains ruler by-means-of council

**Natural reading:** RoundTable does not abolish rulership; it contains rulership by means of council.

**Notes:** The strongest Tonesu-native reading is bounded rule, not flat equality of office. The ruler remains, but the institution places form around power.

---

## KAR-002 Batch Summary

**Entries:** S1024–S1028 · **New vocabulary:** none

| Entry | Source focus | Tonesu | Key feature |

| S1024 (KAR-002-A) | council established | `la-na Arthur ka-be lo-na RoundTable / lo-na RoundTable ne li-pu` | Round Table treated as institution |
| S1025 (KAR-002-B) | equal standing | `la-na RoundTable be lo-li-pu-li su-ne` | `su-ne` marks relational parity |
| S1026 (KAR-002-C) | king plus equal council | `la-na Arthur ne ra-su-li / la-li-pu ne su-ne` | rule and parity coexist |

**Key structural findings:**

**Finding 1: the Round Table is a governance-form before it is an image.** What matters is not the table as object but the council relation it institutes.

**Finding 2: Tonesu can distinguish office from standing.** Arthur remains `ra-su-li`, while the council is `su-ne`. Those are different claims, and the legend depends on keeping both.

**Finding 3: shared deliberation is structurally visible.** S1027 makes the political reciprocity explicit: rule is not represented as a silent ornament of hierarchy.

**Finding 4: the strongest reading is bounded kingship, not proto-democracy.** The Round Table does not negate rule; it contains rule in counsel and prevents visible precedence from becoming the whole constitution.

## King Arthur — Betrayal and Court Fracture (S1029–S1033)

**Batch:** KAR-003  
**Source:** Later Arthurian tradition focusing on Mordred as an internal betrayer and on the failure of the Round Table's counsel-form.  
**Entries:** S1029–S1033 · **New vocabulary:** none

**Key tests:**
- `to-no-si` for secrecy inside a governance structure
- `ka-ne-de` for betrayal as deliberate bond dissolution
- collapse of `su-ne` equal standing under hidden disloyalty
- loss of reciprocal counsel between ruler and council
- distinction between surviving visible form and decayed institution

**Central claim:** The Round Table does not fail because equal counsel is impossible. It fails when secrecy and betrayal dissolve the bonds that equal counsel depends on. Tonesu can state that directly by moving from `su-ne` and reciprocal speech to `to-no-si`, `ka-ne-de`, and finally institutional decay.

---

### S1029 — KAR-003-A: Secrecy enters the council

**Source:** "Mordred willed secrecy within the court."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Mordred  wi  lo-to-no-si`

**Written:** `lana Mordred wi lotonosi`

**Gloss:** Mordred wills secret

**Natural reading:** Mordred wills secrecy.

**Notes:** `to-no-si` is enough to start the failure. A council-form depends on shareable speech; secrecy is already a structural break inside reciprocal counsel.

---

### S1030 — KAR-003-B: Betrayal of the ruler

**Source:** "Mordred betrayed the ruler."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Mordred  ka-ne-de  lo-ne-ra-su-li`

**Written:** `lana Mordred kanede lonerasuli`

**Gloss:** Mordred deliberately-dissolves-bond of-ruler

**Natural reading:** Mordred betrays the ruler.

**Notes:** `ka-ne-de` gives the failure its proper shape: not accidental drift, but chosen bond-dissolution. In Arthurian context the ruler is Arthur, but the political role matters more than the personal name here.

---

### S1031 — KAR-003-C: Equal standing collapses under secrecy and betrayal

**Source:** "Because secrecy and betrayal entered the court, the council no longer stood in equality."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-na Mordred  wi  lo-to-no-si  ;  la-na Mordred  ka-ne-de  lo-ne-ra-su-li},  la-li-pu  no  ne  su-ne`

**Written:** `go {lana Mordred wi lotonosi ; lana Mordred kanede lonerasuli}, lalipu no ne sune`

**Gloss:** because {Mordred wills secret ; Mordred deliberately-dissolves-bond of-ruler}, council not is equal-relation

**Natural reading:** Because Mordred wills secrecy and betrays the ruler, the council no longer stands in equal relation.

**Notes:** This is the Round Table pressure test. `su-ne` was never self-sustaining; it required trust and public reciprocity. Once betrayal enters, the parity-form breaks.

---

### S1032 — KAR-003-D: Reciprocal counsel fails

**Source:** "The council no longer spoke to Arthur, and Arthur no longer spoke to the council."

**Tonesu:** `la-li-pu  no  ka-si  lo-na Arthur  ;  la-na Arthur  no  ka-si  lo-li-pu`

**Written:** `lalipu no kasi lona Arthur ; lana Arthur no kasi lolipu`

**Gloss:** council not speaks-to Arthur ; Arthur not speaks-to council

**Natural reading:** The council no longer speaks to Arthur, and Arthur no longer speaks to the council.

**Notes:** KAR-002's reciprocal speech has now inverted. The institution fails visibly when deliberation stops moving in both directions.

---

### S1033 — KAR-003-E: The form survives, the institution decays

**Source:** "The Round Table does not decay; the council decays."

**Tonesu:** `lo-na RoundTable  no  de  /  lo-li-pu  de`

**Written:** `lona RoundTable no de / lolipu de`

**Gloss:** RoundTable not decays / council decays

**Natural reading:** RoundTable does not decay; the council decays.

**Notes:** This is the Tonesu-native payoff. The visible form can remain while the governing relation fails. The problem is not roundness; the problem is broken counsel.

---

## KAR-003 Batch Summary

**Entries:** S1029–S1033 · **New vocabulary:** none

| Entry | Source focus | Tonesu | Key feature |

**Finding 4: Tonesu separates constitutional form from institutional health.** S1033 states that directly: the Round Table as visible form may remain, while the council as political relation decays.

## King Arthur — Final Battle and Fall (S1034–S1038)

**Batch:** KAR-004  
**Source:** Later Arthurian tradition focusing on Camlann as the terminal conversion of institutional fracture into open combat and political collapse.  
**Entries:** S1034–S1038 · **New vocabulary:** none

**Key tests:**
- `ka-ra` for open combat after counsel fails
- `ka-de-zo` for deliberate killing in the battle register
- `de-zo` for terminal death rather than mere defeat
- separation of battlefield outcome from constitutional outcome
- governance collapse as the end-state after ruler death and council decay

**Central claim:** Arthurian tragedy does not end with betrayal. It ends when broken counsel becomes open combat, combat becomes mutual killing, and the death of the ruler leaves governance itself in decay. Tonesu can state that whole progression without mystifying it.

---

### S1034 — KAR-004-A: Open combat begins

**Source:** "Arthur fought Mordred, and Mordred fought Arthur."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Arthur  ka-ra  lo-na Mordred  ;  la-na Mordred  ka-ra  lo-na Arthur`

**Written:** `lana Arthur kara lona Mordred ; lana Mordred kara lona Arthur`

**Gloss:** Arthur combats Mordred ; Mordred combats Arthur

**Natural reading:** Arthur fights Mordred, and Mordred fights Arthur.

**Notes:** This is the open-war threshold. The failed court relation has now become direct force between named agents.

---

### S1035 — KAR-004-B: Civil conflict becomes killing

**Source:** "Because combat had entered the realm, persons killed persons."

**Tonesu:** `go {ka-ra},  la-o-li  ka-de-zo  lo-li`

**Written:** `go {kara}, laoli kadezo loli`

**Gloss:** because {combat}, collective-persons deliberately-kill persons

**Natural reading:** Because combat has come, persons kill persons.

**Notes:** This is the civil-war pivot. Once the political form has collapsed into combat, killing is no longer exceptional but structurally normal inside the polity.

---

### S1036 — KAR-004-C: Mordred kills Arthur

**Source:** "Mordred killed Arthur."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Mordred  ka-de-zo  lo-na Arthur`

**Written:** `lana Mordred kadezo lona Arthur`

**Gloss:** Mordred deliberately-kills Arthur

**Natural reading:** Mordred kills Arthur.

**Notes:** The betrayal sequence reaches its terminal act here. What began as hidden bond-dissolution ends as direct organism-decrease.

---

### S1037 — KAR-004-D: Arthur kills Mordred and dies

**Source:** "Arthur killed Mordred, and Arthur died."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Arthur  ka-de-zo  lo-na Mordred  ;  la-na Arthur  ne  de-zo`

**Written:** `lana Arthur kadezo lona Mordred ; lana Arthur ne dezo`

**Gloss:** Arthur deliberately-kills Mordred ; Arthur is death

**Natural reading:** Arthur kills Mordred, and Arthur dies.

**Notes:** The Tonesu distinction matters: `ka-de-zo` marks the intentional killing of Mordred; `de-zo` marks Arthur's terminal death as the resulting condition.

---

### S1038 — KAR-004-E: Governance collapses after the ruler's death

**Source:** "Because Arthur died and the council had already decayed, governance itself decayed."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-na Arthur  ne  de-zo  ;  lo-li-pu  de},  lo-ka-li-su  de`

**Written:** `go {lana Arthur ne dezo ; lolipu de}, lokalisu de`

**Gloss:** because {Arthur is death ; council decays}, governance decays

**Natural reading:** Because Arthur dies and the council decays, governance decays.

**Notes:** This is the final constitutional verdict. The story does not end with a duel; it ends with the decay of governance after the ruler is gone and the deliberative institution has already failed.

---

## KAR-004 Batch Summary

**Entries:** S1034–S1038 · **New vocabulary:** none

| Entry | Source focus | Tonesu | Key feature |
|-------|--------------|--------|-------------|
| S1034 (KAR-004-A) | final battle opens | `la-na Arthur ka-ra lo-na Mordred ; la-na Mordred ka-ra lo-na Arthur` | combat becomes explicit |
| S1035 (KAR-004-B) | civil war | `go {ka-ra}, la-o-li ka-de-zo lo-li` | combat normalizes killing |
| S1036 (KAR-004-C) | Arthur falls | `la-na Mordred ka-de-zo lo-na Arthur` | betrayal culminates in killing |
| S1037 (KAR-004-D) | Mordred falls, Arthur dies | `la-na Arthur ka-de-zo lo-na Mordred ; la-na Arthur ne de-zo` | kill vs death distinguished |
| S1038 (KAR-004-E) | rule collapses | `go {la-na Arthur ne de-zo ; lo-li-pu de}, lo-ka-li-su de` | governance decay as terminal outcome |

**Key structural findings:**

**Finding 1: the last Arthur step is not just battle but conversion of politics into combat.** Once counsel fails, force becomes the only remaining grammar of rule.

**Finding 2: Tonesu keeps killing and death distinct.** `ka-de-zo` names deliberate lethal action; `de-zo` names the terminal death-state that follows.

**Finding 3: civil war is structurally clearer than heroic duel.** S1035 makes the important claim: when combat enters the polity, persons kill persons inside the same social field.

**Finding 4: the true ending is governance decay.** The Arthurian fall is complete only when the ruler dies and `lo-ka-li-su` itself decays.
