## D&D — Twenty Core Spells (S976–S995)

**Batch:** DND-006  
**Source:** Dungeons & Dragons core spell list, selecting twenty iconic spells across the major operator families already established in DND-001 and DND-005. Tests whether Tonesu can describe individual spells as effect-patterns rather than as arbitrary fantasy labels.  
**Entries:** S976–S995 · **New vocabulary:** none (all compositional)

**Key tests:**
- operator-family reuse at the spell level: `ko-fe-ki`, `pa-be-ki`, `ra-be-ki`, `to-se-ki`, `ge-se-ki`, `wi-fa-ki`, `de-zo-ki`, `ge-ki`
- named-spell analysis without coining a new spell-name lexicon
- distinction between real force (`ra-be`) and apparent form (`ge-se`)
- distinction between thought/perception effects and affect/will effects
- proof that the five-family redesign supports downstream spell description better than the old flat school list

**Central claim:** A spell list is a better test of Tonesu magic than school names alone because spells force the taxonomy to cash out in actual effects. If the operator families are real, they should support a healthy list of iconic spells without needing ad hoc vocabulary for every entry.

---

### S976 — DND-006-A: Shield

**Source:** "Shield" — an instantaneous magical barrier that blocks incoming force.

**Tonesu:** `la-na Shield  ne  ko-fe-ki  /  ko  lo-ra-be`

**Written:** `lana Shield ne kofeki / ko lorabe`

**Gloss:** Shield-[named] IS abjuration / contains manifest-force

**Natural reading:** Shield is a boundary art / it contains incoming force.

**Notes:** This is the cleanest abjuration spell in the list. The spell does not destroy force; it contains it. That matches the DND-001 analysis exactly: abjuration adds a boundary rather than subtracting a threat.

---

### S977 — DND-006-B: Mage Armor

**Source:** "Mage Armor" — a protective magical boundary placed around the body.

**Tonesu:** `la-na MageArmor  ne  ko-fe-ki  /  ko  lo-ze  go-si  ko-fe`

**Written:** `lana MageArmor ne kofeki / ko loze gosi kofe`

**Gloss:** Mage-Armor-[named] IS abjuration / contains person by-means-of ward-boundary

**Natural reading:** Mage Armor is a boundary art / it encloses the person within a ward-boundary.

**Notes:** The spell differs from Shield by persistence and target, not by operator family. Both are `ko-fe-ki`; Mage Armor simply fixes the protective limit around the body instead of intercepting an incoming strike.

### S978 — DND-006-C: Counterspell

**Finding 2: iconic spells do not require a special spell-name lexicon.** The named spell can remain a proper-name anchor while the analysis cashes out in ordinary operator-family vocabulary.

**Finding 3: spell effects clarify the DND-005 repair.** The old eight-school list was abstractly debatable. Individual spell effects make the repair concrete: Counterspell is obviously about limit-governing, Fireball is obviously about force-emergence, Charm Person is obviously about affect-will, and Polymorph is obviously about property-change.

**Finding 4: necromancy is still just a domain-specialized alteration family.** Speak with Dead and Animate Dead do not require a distinct magical operator. They remain `de-zo-ki` cases: alteration at the death-boundary.

## D&D — Civic and Worldbuilding Spells (S996–S1015)

**Batch:** DND-007  
**Source:** Dungeons & Dragons utility and infrastructure-facing spells, selecting twenty effects that matter for communication, shelter, law, agriculture, travel, restoration, and social order.  
**Entries:** S996–S1015 · **New vocabulary:** none (all compositional)

**Key tests:**
- whether the DND-005 operator families remain productive outside direct combat
- magical infrastructure: signaling, warding, shelter, travel, lighting, translation, agriculture, and resurrection
- spell effects that matter for cities, temples, courts, farms, and frontier life rather than only dungeon encounters
- distinction between communication, perception, will-binding, and alteration of living conditions

**Central claim:** If a magic system is going to matter for world building, it has to describe the spells that reshape ordinary institutions. A spell language that only handles Fireball and Polymorph is incomplete. DND-007 therefore pressures Tonesu magic where cultures, economies, and governments would actually feel it.

## King Arthur — Sword in the Stone (S1016–S1023)

**Batch:** KAR-001  
**Source:** Arthurian sword-in-the-stone episode in the Malory/traditional register, focusing on the public sign of kingship rather than battlefield heroics.  
**Entries:** S1016–S1023 · **New vocabulary:** none

**Key tests:**
- legitimacy through public sign rather than bloodline exposition alone
- `si` versus `to` for first-attested public evidence versus established political conclusion
- `ru-fe,` as an exclusive accession criterion
- repetition and public witness as the bridge from event to accepted rule
- `wi-fe'ka-to-fe` as the clean way to describe the community's act of classifying someone as ruler

**Central claim:** The sword-in-the-stone story is not mainly about strength. It is a compact theory of legitimacy: a public sign appears, the sign is doubted, the sign is repeated before witnesses, and only then is kingship treated as established. That sequence is exactly the kind of distinction Tonesu can make explicit.

---

### S1016 — KAR-001-A: Exclusive accession criterion

**Source:** "Whoever draws the sword is the rightful king."

**Tonesu:** `ru-fe,  la-ze  ki  lo-na SwordInStone  ne  ra-su-li`

**Written:** `rufe, laze ki lona SwordInStone ne rasuli`

**Gloss:** only, person moves name SwordInStone is ruler

**Natural reading:** Only the person who draws SwordInStone is ruler.

**Notes:** The important feature is not the object itself but the exclusive criterion. The tale makes accession depend on one public test rather than on diffuse noble preference.

---

### S1017 — KAR-001-B: Others fail the criterion

**Source:** "The others could not draw it."

**Tonesu:** `la-o-li  no  ki  lo-na SwordInStone`

**Written:** `laoli no ki lona SwordInStone`

**Gloss:** collective-persons not move name SwordInStone

**Natural reading:** The others do not draw SwordInStone.

**Notes:** The criterion matters because it excludes. Without the failure of others, the sign would not function politically.

---

### S1018 — KAR-001-C: Arthur performs the act

**Source:** "Arthur drew the sword."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Arthur  ki  lo-na SwordInStone`

**Written:** `lana Arthur ki lona SwordInStone`

**Gloss:** Arthur moves name SwordInStone

**Natural reading:** Arthur draws SwordInStone.

**Notes:** This is the bare event. By itself it is only the first half of legitimacy.

---

### S1019 — KAR-001-D: Signal without full establishment

**Source:** "The people saw the sign, but did not yet hold Arthur's kingship as established."

**Tonesu:** `la-o-li  si  [la-na Arthur  ki  lo-na SwordInStone]  /  no-to  [la-na Arthur  ne  ra-su-li]`

**Written:** `laoli si [lana Arthur ki lona SwordInStone] / noto [lana Arthur ne rasuli]`

**Gloss:** collective-persons signal-level [Arthur moves name SwordInStone] / not-established [Arthur is ruler]

**Natural reading:** The people have a signal that Arthur drew SwordInStone, but they do not yet hold it as established that Arthur is ruler.

**Notes:** This is the key pressure point in the episode. The event is visible, but the political conclusion is not automatic.

---

### S1020 — KAR-001-E: Demand for repetition

**Source:** "The people called for Arthur to do it again."

**Tonesu:** `la-o-li  ka-si  lo-na Arthur  /  wi  [la-na Arthur  ki  lo-na SwordInStone  re]`

**Written:** `laoli kasi lona Arthur / wi [lana Arthur ki lona SwordInStone re]`

**Gloss:** collective-persons speak to Arthur / in-order-that [Arthur moves name SwordInStone again]

**Natural reading:** The people call to Arthur so that he will draw SwordInStone again.

**Notes:** Repetition is not decorative. It is the community's attempt to turn a surprising event into a credible public criterion.

---

### S1021 — KAR-001-F: Repeated public act

**Source:** "Arthur drew it again, and the people saw him."

**Tonesu:** `la-na Arthur  ki  lo-na SwordInStone  re  ;  la-o-li  se  lo-na Arthur`

**Written:** `lana Arthur ki lona SwordInStone re ; laoli se lona Arthur`

**Gloss:** Arthur moves name SwordInStone again ; collective-persons perceive Arthur

**Natural reading:** Arthur draws SwordInStone again, and the people see him.

**Notes:** The public-witness dimension enters here. The second act is socially stronger than the first because it is jointly seen.

---

### S1022 — KAR-001-G: Public establishment

**Source:** "After the repeated public act, the people held that Arthur was king."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-na Arthur  ki  lo-na SwordInStone  re  ;  la-o-li  se  lo-na Arthur},  la-o-li  to  [la-na Arthur  ne  ra-su-li]`

**Written:** `go {lana Arthur ki lona SwordInStone re ; laoli se lona Arthur}, laoli to [lana Arthur ne rasuli]`

**Gloss:** because {Arthur moves name SwordInStone again ; collective-persons perceive Arthur}, collective-persons establish [Arthur is ruler]

**Natural reading:** Because Arthur draws SwordInStone again and the people witness him, the people hold it established that Arthur is ruler.

**Notes:** This is where `si` becomes `to`. The causal structure matters: public repetition converts sign into accepted conclusion.

---

### S1023 — KAR-001-H: Category assignment, not magical essence

**Source:** "The sword does not create kingship; the people classify Arthur as ruler through it."

**Tonesu:** `la-na SwordInStone  no  be  lo-ra-su-li  /  la-o-li  wi-fe'ka-to-fe  lo-na Arthur  ne  ra-su-li`

**Written:** `lana SwordInStone no be lorasuli / laoli wife'katofe lona Arthur ne rasuli`

**Gloss:** SwordInStone not produces ruler / collective-persons norm-categorize Arthur as ruler

**Natural reading:** SwordInStone does not create a ruler; the people classify Arthur as ruler through it.

**Notes:** This is the Tonesu-native payoff. The tale is not about magical essence transfer. It is about a public sign licensing a political category.

## KAR-001 Batch Summary

**Entries:** S1016–S1023 · **New vocabulary:** none

| Entry | Source focus | Tonesu | Key feature |

**Key structural findings:**

**Finding 1: the Arthurian scene is epistemic before it is heroic.** The real pressure is not "can someone move the sword?" but "when does a witnessed act count as enough to establish rule?"

**Finding 2: repetition is politically load-bearing.** The second drawing is not redundant. It is the mechanism that turns a surprising event into a shared public proof.

**Finding 3: Tonesu cleanly separates sign from accepted legitimacy.** S1019 and S1022 show the full movement from `si` to `to`: visible signal first, established collective conclusion later.

**Finding 4: kingship is treated as a category assigned by the polity, not a mystical substance emitted by the sword.** `wi-fe'ka-to-fe` states that directly and more honestly than the legend's surface phrasing.
