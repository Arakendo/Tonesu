## LSE-001 — Lease Entry Notice Clause (S922–S924)

**Batch:** LSE-001  
**Source:** Lease-entry notice clause, paraphrasing the standard rule that a party may not enter a dwelling without prior notice and may enter only for limited purposes after the wait period. Tests whether Tonesu can separate notice, elapsed time, and permitted purpose rather than packing them into a single English legal phrase.  
**Entries:** S922–S924 · **New vocabulary:** none (all compositional)

**Key tests:**
- `si-de` — recorded notice as the contract-facing notice form
- `bun mol nu re-ti` — exact 24-hour duration in legal register
- `wi-fe-ka [clause]` / `no-wi-fe-ka [clause]` — prohibited vs permitted entry as clause-level deontic states
- `ne-to-fe` + `no-wi-fe-ka` — contract as the source of permitted entry

**Central claim:** English lease language compresses three distinct things into one clause: notice, elapsed wait, and limited purpose. Tonesu is clearer when these are split. The batch therefore does not lexicalize "landlord" as a special role; it keeps the legal relation explicit and tests the clause mechanics directly.

---

### S922 — LSE-001-A

**Source:** "If a party gives no recorded notice, entry is forbidden."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ze  no  si  lo-si-de},  wi-fe-ka  [la-ze  ki  lo-ko-pa]`

**Written:** `go {laze no si loside}, wifeka [laze ki lokopa]`

**Gloss:** because [other-particular-person NOT signals record] [rule-forbidden] [that-person enters enclosed-space]

**Natural reading:** If the party gives no recorded notice, entry into the room is forbidden.

**Notes:** `si-de` (W098) is used here as recorded notice: not merely a spoken warning but a signal that has entered the record. The batch starts with the negative form because lease clauses typically define permission through a prior prohibition condition. `wi-fe-ka [la-ze ki lo-ko-pa]` keeps the deontic claim at clause level, following KAF-001 and PERM-001: what matters is not physical inability but normative prohibition of entry.

---

### S923 — LSE-001-B

**Source:** "Once recorded notice is given and 24 hours pass, entry is permitted."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ze  si  lo-si-de ; bun mol  nu  re-ti  ki},  no-wi-fe-ka  [la-ze  ki  lo-ko-pa]`

**Written:** `go {laze si loside ; bun mol nu reti ki}, nowifeka [laze ki lokopa]`

**Gloss:** because [other-particular-person signals record ; twenty-four quantity-of-hour moves] [not-rule-forbidden] [that-person enters enclosed-space]

**Natural reading:** Once the party gives recorded notice and twenty-four hours pass, entry into the room is permitted.

**Notes:** `bun mol nu re-ti` gives the literal legal duration: twenty-four hours. The batch treats elapsed time as its own clause (`bun mol nu re-ti ki`) rather than hiding it inside a stacked prepositional phrase. That is the point of the test: Tonesu legal prose prefers discrete conditions over compressed English clause-bundles. `no-wi-fe-ka` confirms that permission is modeled as absence of prohibition, exactly as PERM-001 established.

---

### S924 — LSE-001-C

**Source:** "The contract permits entry for inspection or repair."

**Tonesu:** `la-ne-to-fe  ne  no-wi-fe-ka  [la-ze  ki  lo-ko-pa  wi {ka-se  lo-ko-pa / ka-de-be  lo-ko-pa}]`

**Written:** `lanetofe ne nowifeka [laze ki lokopa wi {kase lokopa / kadebe lokopa}]`

**Gloss:** [contract] IS [not-rule-forbidden] [other-particular-person enters enclosed-space in-order-that {inspects enclosed-space / repairs enclosed-space}]

**Natural reading:** The contract permits a party to enter the room in order to inspect or repair it.

**Notes:** `ne-to-fe` (W165) is extended from covenant/treaty into ordinary contract register: a formally bounded relation with stated terms. The purpose frame `wi {ka-se ... / ka-de-be ...}` supplies the limited purposes instead of relying on lexical implication. This is where the clause becomes recognizably contractual rather than merely deontic: the contract does not grant general presence, but bounded permission tied to inspection or repair.

## GDPR-001 — GDPR Article 6(1): Lawful Bases for Processing (S925–S931)

**Batch:** GDPR-001  
**Source:** GDPR Article 6(1), paraphrasing the six lawful bases for processing personal data. Tests whether Tonesu can render the list as a battery of permissions rather than a disguised ontological taxonomy, and whether the overlap among the categories remains visible.  
**Entries:** S925–S931 · **New vocabulary:** none (all compositional)

**Key tests:**
- `lo-ka-to-ki lo-to-si ne i-zo-li` — act of processing personal data as a repeated legal object
- `ne-vo-wi` / `ne-to-fe` / `wi-fe` / `wi-ra` / `~vo-ne` — the lawful-basis battery
- `no-wi-fe-ka` — lawful basis as permission, not obligation
- list-structure verdict on the overlap between the categories

**Central claim:** GDPR 6(1) is not a list of six different kinds of truth. It is a list of six different ways a rule may suspend prohibition over the same act. Tonesu keeps that permission-structure explicit and therefore also exposes the statute's fuzzy catch-all: "legitimate interests" is the only basis that arrives already carrying `~`.

---

### S925 — GDPR-001-A

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if the data subject has given consent."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-i-zo-li  ne  ne-vo-wi  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {laizoli ne nevowi lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [particular-person IS consent-relation to act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When the person consents to the processing of that person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** `ne-vo-wi` (consent) is treated as a relation attaching to the act, not merely an emotional state. That matters legally: the basis is not "the person approves in general" but "the person stands in a consent relation to this act." `no-wi-fe-ka` keeps the article's logic correct: consent does not compel processing; it suspends prohibition.

---

### S926 — GDPR-001-B

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for the performance of a contract."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ne-to-fe  ne  wi-fe  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {lanetofe ne wife lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [contract IS rule over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When a contract requires the processing of a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** `ne-to-fe` is the formally bounded agreement. The article's contract basis is rendered here as the contract holding rule over the processing act. This is more precise than saying the contract merely "mentions" the act: the contract is the normative source making the act permissible.

---

### S927 — GDPR-001-C

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-wi-fe-su  ne  wi-fe  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {lawifesu ne wife lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [rule-structure IS rule over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When a legal rule requires the processing of a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** This basis looks superficially close to S926, but the source of normativity is different. In S926 the bounded agreement (`ne-to-fe`) is load-bearing; here the institutional rule-system (`wi-fe-su`) is. GDPR's list thereby distinguishes private bounded agreement from public rule structure, even when the resulting permission looks identical at the surface.

---

### S928 — GDPR-001-D

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary in order to protect vital interests."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-i-zo-li  zo-ra  de},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {laizoli zora de}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [particular-person vitality decreases] [act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When a person's vitality is in danger, the processing of that person's personal data is permitted.

**Notes:** `zo-ra` (vitality / life-energy) keeps the article's emergency basis grounded in organism-level preservation rather than abstraction. GDPR's phrasing says "vital interests"; the batch translates that as threat to animating life-condition. This is the clearest basis in the article: unlike consent or legitimate interests, the justification is not category-disputable once the emergency is real.

---

### S929 — GDPR-001-E

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ka-li-su  ne  wi-ra  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {lakalisu ne wira lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [governance IS authority over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When governance has official authority over the processing of a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** This is the article's public-task / official-authority arm rendered through `wi-ra`, not bare `wi-fe`. The distinction matters: `wi-fe` marks rule/obligation; `wi-ra` marks authorized exercise of office. GDPR keeps those apart, and the batch preserves the split.

---

### S930 — GDPR-001-F

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ze  ne  ~vo-ne  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {laze ne ~vone lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [other-particular-person IS ~rightful-standing over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When another party has `~rightful-interest` in processing a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** This is the article's catch-all basis, and it is the only one that arrives already carrying `~`. `~vo-ne` marks the phrase as structurally vague in the source system, not merely uncertain to the speaker. "Legitimate interests" is not a clean legal kind in the way consent or contract are. It is a source-system approximation whose boundary is intentionally left soft.

---

### S931 — GDPR-001-G

**Source:** Tonesu structural verdict on Article 6(1)

**Tonesu:** `la-wi-fe  ne  no-su-to  /  ke,  la-wi-fe  no  to-su-ki  lo-to-fe  {ne-vo-wi ; ne-to-fe ; wi-fe ; wi-ra ; ~vo-ne}`

**Written:** `lawife ne nosuto / ke, lawife no tosuki lotofe {nevowi ; netofe ; wife ; wira ; ~vone}`

**Gloss:** [the rule] IS [not-self-evident-truth] / but [the rule] NOT comprehends [the boundary] {consent ; contract ; rule ; authority ; ~rightful-interest}

**Natural reading:** The rule is not a self-evident truth / but the rule has not comprehended the boundary among consent, contract, legal obligation, authority, and `~legitimate-interest`.

**Notes:** Article 6(1) is a workable legal battery, not a coherent taxonomy. The sentence mirrors the statute's actual condition: the list functions, but its members are not all of one kind and they overlap structurally. `~vo-ne` is the most obvious outlier, but the real issue is broader: the article gives multiple ways to suspend prohibition without making their boundary relations explicit.

## Robotics Law Battery (S941-S950) *(ROB-001)*

**Purpose:** Tests two adjacent things at once: first, whether Tonesu can state the classic robotics-law hierarchy cleanly without inventing a special "robot" lexeme; second, whether Tonesu's own distinctions among prohibition, authority, and epistemic grade produce a better law-set than a straight English inheritance.

---

**S941 — Classic Law 1a: machine may not harm a person.** *(ROB-001-A)*

```
wi-fe-ka  [la-mu  ka-de  lo-i-zo-li]
```

Written: `wifeka [lamu kade loizoli]`

**Notes:** This is the cleanest direct paraphrase of the first half of Asimov's first law. `wi-fe-ka [clause]` keeps the rule at clause level: what is prohibited is not the machine as such but the act of deliberate harm against a particular person. `mu` is sufficient here; the pressure is on law architecture, not on inventing a dedicated humanoid-machine lexeme.

---

**S942 — Classic Law 1b: danger to a person's life creates a rescue duty.** *(ROB-001-B)*

```
go {la-i-zo-li  zo-ra  de},  la-mu  ne-fe  lo-ka-no-de  lo-i-zo-li
```

Written: `go {laizoli zora de}, lamu nefe lokanode loizoli`

**Notes:** The English source famously hides the inaction clause inside the word "allow." Tonesu forces the duty out into its own sentence: if a person's life-energy is under threat, the machine is required to preserve that person. `ka-no-de` (deliberate preservation) gives the rescue side explicitly instead of implying it through the negation of harm.

---

**S943 — Classic Law 2: a human demand binds unless the first law blocks it.** *(ROB-001-C)*

```
go {la-i-zo-li  wi-ra  [la-mu  wi-ka] ; no-wi-fe-ka  [la-mu  ka-de  lo-i-zo-li]},  la-mu  ne-fe  lo-wi-ka
```

Written: `go {laizoli wira [lamu wika] ; nowifeka [lamu kade loizoli]}, lamu nefe lowika`

**Notes:** This is a functional paraphrase of the second law rather than a direct quotation. `wi-ra` is used in its demand predicate sense: the person demands that the machine act. The result clause uses `ne-fe` to convert that demand into a requirement on the machine, but only after the no-harm condition is checked.

**S944 — Classic Law 3: self-preservation is subordinate.** *(ROB-001-D)*

```

Written: `go {lomu nefe lonode ; nowifeka [lamu kade loizoli] ; no laizoli wira [lamu wika]}, lamu node`

**Notes:** The third law is rendered as a dependency condition, not as a machine's pseudo-instinct. `lo-mu ne-fe lo-no-de` = the machine requires preservation/continued integrity. The act of self-preservation is then allowed only when neither person-safety nor a current human demand blocks it.

**S945 — Structural verdict on the classic hierarchy.** *(ROB-001-E)*

```

Written: `lawifesu ne nosuto / ke, lawifesu no tosuki lotofe {wira ; wife ; si ; to}`

**Notes:** The classic hierarchy works as a memorable rule battery, but in Tonesu it reveals its compression cost. It does not fully comprehend the boundaries among demand/authority (`wi-ra`), prohibition (`wi-fe`), and epistemic grade (`si` vs `to`). That is the fault-line the redesigned laws address.

**S946 — Tonesu-native Law 1: persons are rights-bearers, so machine harm is forbidden.** *(ROB-001-F)*

la-i-zo-li  ne  wi-fe  /  wi-fe-ka  [la-mu  ka-de  lo-i-zo-li]
```

Written: `laizoli ne wife / wifeka [lamu kade loizoli]`

**Notes:** The redesign starts by making the normative basis explicit: the person is not merely a protected biological object but a rights-bearer. The prohibition on machine harm then follows from that status instead of standing as an unexplained species preference.

---

**S947 — Tonesu-native Law 2: rescue duty is stated separately from the harm ban.** *(ROB-001-G)*

```
go {la-i-zo-li  zo-ra  de},  la-mu  ne-fe  lo-ka-no-de  lo-i-zo-li
```

Written: `go {laizoli zora de}, lamu nefe lokanode loizoli`

**Notes:** This repeats the rescue clause because the redesign keeps it as a first-class law rather than leaving it buried inside a negated-harm formula. Tonesu's compositional system is more honest when prohibition and required preservation are kept distinct.

**S948 — Tonesu-native Law 3: only recognized authority can issue binding demands to a machine.** *(ROB-001-H)*

```
go {la-i-zo-li  ne  wi-ra  lo-mu ; la-i-zo-li  wi-ra  [la-mu  wi-ka] ; no-wi-fe-ka  [la-mu  ka-de  lo-i-zo-li]},  la-mu  ne-fe  lo-wi-ka

**Notes:** This is the first substantive improvement over the classic second law. Mere human demand is no longer enough. The speaker must stand in an explicit authority relation to the machine, and the demanded act remains subordinate to the person-safety rule.

**S949 — Tonesu-native Law 4: signal-grade danger without knowledge-grade certainty must be escalated.** *(ROB-001-I)*

**S950 — Tonesu-native Law 5: self-maintenance is a dependency condition subordinate to person-safety and authority.** *(ROB-001-J)*

**Notes:** The machine's own integrity is still protected, but the law states the condition in dependency terms (`ne-fe`) rather than in anthropomorphic "self-preservation" language. ## Psycho-Pass — Crime Coefficient and Unpublished Thresholds (S963-S968) *(PSY-001)*

**Purpose:** Extends the predictive-policing sequence from future-action forecasts to black-box risk classification. This batch tests whether Tonesu can represent a system that classifies persons by thresholded affect-signal and then enforces that classification without publishing the standard that authorizes it.

---

**S963 — The system signals a person's hostile arousal above threshold.** *(PSY-001-A)*

```
la-na Sibyl  si  [lo-na Kogami  fa-ra-be  nu-be  lo-si-fe]
```

Written: `lana Sibyl si [lona Kogami farabe nube losife]`

**Notes:** The first Psycho-Pass move is not guilt but thresholded signal. `fa-ra-be nu-be lo-si-fe` keeps the claim in the domain of measured affect-risk crossing a limit, not a proved completed wrong.

---

**S964 — Classification precedes detention.** *(PSY-001-B)*

```
go {la-na Sibyl  si  [lo-na Kogami  fa-ra-be  nu-be  lo-si-fe]},  la-o-ka-su  su-ka  lo-na Kogami ; la-o-ka-su  ka-ko  lo-na Kogami
```

Written: `go {lana Sibyl si [lona Kogami farabe nube losife]}, laokasu suka lona Kogami ; laokasu kako lona Kogami`

**Notes:** This is the Psycho-Pass protocol in Tonesu form: threshold signal, then structural classification, then custody. `su-ka` matters here because the system is not reading a completed act; it is imposing a category on a person and letting that category drive confinement.

---

**S965 — High coefficient is not present fault.** *(PSY-001-C)*

```
la-na Sibyl  si  [lo-na Kogami  fa-ra-be  nu-be  lo-si-fe]  /  lo-na Kogami  no  ne  de-su
```

Written: `lana Sibyl si [lona Kogami farabe nube losife] / lona Kogami no ne desu`

**Notes:** This is the batch's central correction. A thresholded danger-signal is not yet fault. Tonesu refuses the Psycho-Pass move where score, category, and guilt silently merge.

---

**S966 — An unpublished threshold standard cannot bind.** *(PSY-001-D)*

```
go {lo-to-fe-su  no  to-fe-su-ki},  wi-fe-ka  [la-o-ka-su  ka-ko  lo-na Kogami]
```

Written: `go {lotofesu no tofesuki}, wifeka [laokasu kako lona Kogami]`

**Notes:** This is the publication-state gap made concrete. If the standard has not crossed into published `to-su` status, it has no legitimate binding force. PSY-001 therefore activates `to-fe-su-ki` exactly where the repo's open question predicted it would matter.

---

**S967 — Unpublished threshold plus lethal enforcement is epistemic abuse.** *(PSY-001-E)*

```
go {lo-to-fe-su  no  to-fe-su-ki ; la-o-ka-su  ka-de  lo-na Kogami  go-si  [la-na Sibyl  si  [lo-na Kogami  fa-ra-be  nu-be  lo-si-fe]]},  la-o-ka-su  ne  to-fe-ka
```

Written: `go {lotofesu no tofesuki ; laokasu kade lona Kogami gosi [lana Sibyl si [lona Kogami farabe nube losife]]}, laokasu ne tofeka`

**Notes:** This is the hardest legal claim in the batch. If the apparatus kills on the basis of a thresholded signal whose authorizing standard was never published, the regime is not merely severe; it is committing epistemic inflation at the meta-level. It treats internal adoption as if it had certified public force.

---

**S968 — Structural verdict: Psycho-Pass collapses signal, category, and punishment.** *(PSY-001-F)*

```
la-wi-fe-su  no  to-su-ki  lo-to-fe  {si ; su-ka ; ka-ko ; ka-de}  /  ke,  lo-to-fe-su  ne-fe  to-fe-su-ki ; la-o-ka-su  ne-fe  lo-ka-si  lo-to-fe-li
```

Written: `lawifesu no tosuki lotofe {si ; suka ; kako ; kade} / ke, lotofesu nefe tofesuki ; laokasu nefe lokaci lotofeli`

**Notes:** Psycho-Pass is not just a surveillance story. It is a category-collapse story: signal becomes classification, classification becomes custody, and custody can become death. Tonesu breaks those steps apart and makes publication plus adjudication the lawful path.

## D&D — Schools of Magic Recategorized (S969-S975) *(DND-005)*

**Purpose:** DND-001 showed that the eight legacy school names translate cleanly. DND-005 asks the harder follow-up question: once the translated compounds are visible, should they still be treated as eight peer categories? The batch tests a Tonesu-native recategorization in which shared heads define operator families and root modifiers define the domain-specializations under them.

---

**S969 — Necromancy is transmutation constrained to the death-domain.** *(DND-005-A)*

```
de-zo-ki  ne  ge-ki  go-si  de-zo
```

Written: `dezoki ne geki gosi dezo`

**Notes:** This makes the key DND-001 finding explicit in sentence form. `de-zo-ki` and `ge-ki` share the same `-ki` head, so necromancy is not a fundamentally different operation from transmutation. It is transmutation operating through the `de-zo` domain: organism-change at the death threshold.

---

**S970 — Conjuration and Evocation are two manifestation arts distinguished by domain.** *(DND-005-B)*

```
pa-be-ki  ne  be-ki  go-si  pa  /  ra-be-ki  ne  be-ki  go-si  ra
```

Written: `pabeki ne beki gosi pa / rabeki ne beki gosi ra`

**Notes:** The shared head `be-ki` is the point. Conjuration and evocation are not unrelated schools; they are parallel manifestation arts. `pa` marks spatial emergence, while `ra` marks force-emergence.

---

**S971 — Divination and Illusion are two perception arts distinguished by aim.** *(DND-005-C)*

```
to-se-ki  ne  se-ki  go-si  to  /  ge-se-ki  ne  se-ki  go-si  ge
```

Written: `toseki ne seki gosi to / geseki ne seki gosi ge`

**Notes:** Again the shared head is decisive. Both schools are `se-ki` arts: transformed perception. The difference is whether perception is being extended toward knowledge (`to`) or altered toward appearance (`ge`).

---

**S972 — Abjuration remains a boundary art.** *(DND-005-D)*

```
ko-fe-ki  ne  fe-ki  go-si  ko
```

Written: `kofeki ne feki gosi ko`

**Notes:** Abjuration survives the recategorization cleanly. It is still best understood as limit-change operating through containment. Unlike necromancy, conjuration, or illusion, it does not collapse into a paired sibling school under the present inventory.

---

**S973 — Enchantment remains an affect-will art.** *(DND-005-E)*

```
wi-fa-ki  ne  fa-ki  go-si  wi
```

Written: `wifaki ne faki gosi wi`

**Notes:** Enchantment also remains coherent as its own family. `fa-ki` gives the affect-shift head, and `wi` specifies that the shift is intentionally imposed through will. The school therefore targets the affect-will substrate rather than space, force, or perception.

---

**S974 — The legacy school list does not comprehend the boundary between operator and domain.** *(DND-005-F)*

```
la-to-su  lo-wi-ra-su  ne  no-su-to  /  ke,  la-to-su  lo-wi-ra-su  no  to-su-ki  lo-to-fe  {ki ; be ; se}  ne  {de-zo ; pa ; ra ; to ; ge}
```

Written: `latosu lowirasu ne nosuto / ke, latosu lowirasu no tosuki lotofe {ki ; be ; se} ne {dezo ; pa ; ra ; to ; ge}`

**Notes:** This is the structural verdict on the eight-school list. The old taxonomy functions, but it is not `su-to`: it mixes operator heads (`ki`, `be`, `se`) with domain modifiers (`de-zo`, `pa`, `ra`, `to`, `ge`) as if they were the same kind of category.

---

**S975 — A better magical curriculum groups domains under shared operator families.** *(DND-005-G)*

```
la-to-su  lo-wi-ra-su  su-ka  lo-pa  ne  lo-be-ki ; su-ka  lo-ra  ne  lo-be-ki ; su-ka  lo-to  ne  lo-se-ki ; su-ka  lo-ge  ne  lo-se-ki ; su-ka  lo-de-zo  ne  lo-ge-ki
```

Written: `latosu lowirasu suka lopa ne lobeki ; suka lora ne lobeki ; suka loto ne loseki ; suka loge ne loseki ; suka lodezo ne logeki`

**Notes:** This is the practical redesign. A better curriculum groups space and force under manifestation art, knowledge and appearance under perception art, and death-domain under transmutation. DND-005 does not invent a new fantasy canon; it simply lets the existing compounds sort themselves honestly.
