## LSE-001 — Lease Entry Notice Clause (S922–S924)

**Batch:** LSE-001  
**Source:** Lease-entry notice clause, paraphrasing the standard rule that a party may not enter a dwelling without prior notice and may enter only for limited purposes after the wait period. Tests whether Tonesu can separate notice, elapsed time, and permitted purpose rather than packing them into a single English legal phrase.  
**Entries:** S922–S924 · **New vocabulary:** none (all compositional)

**Key tests:**
- `si-de` — recorded notice as the contract-facing notice form
- `bun mol nu re-ti` — exact 24-hour duration in legal register
- `wi-fe-ka [clause]` / `no-wi-fe-ka [clause]` — prohibited vs permitted entry as clause-level deontic states
- `ne-to-fe` + `no-wi-fe-ka` — contract as the source of permitted entry

**Central claim:** English lease language compresses three distinct things into one clause: notice, elapsed wait, and limited purpose. Tonesu is clearer when these are split. The batch therefore does not lexicalize "landlord" as a special role; it keeps the legal relation explicit and tests the clause mechanics directly.

---

### S922 — LSE-001-A

**Source:** "If a party gives no recorded notice, entry is forbidden."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ze  no  si  lo-si-de},  wi-fe-ka  [la-ze  ki  lo-ko-pa]`

**Written:** `go {laze no si loside}, wifeka [laze ki lokopa]`

**Gloss:** because [other-particular-person NOT signals record] [rule-forbidden] [that-person enters enclosed-space]

**Natural reading:** If the party gives no recorded notice, entry into the room is forbidden.

**Notes:** `si-de` (W098) is used here as recorded notice: not merely a spoken warning but a signal that has entered the record. The batch starts with the negative form because lease clauses typically define permission through a prior prohibition condition. `wi-fe-ka [la-ze ki lo-ko-pa]` keeps the deontic claim at clause level, following KAF-001 and PERM-001: what matters is not physical inability but normative prohibition of entry.

---

### S923 — LSE-001-B

**Source:** "Once recorded notice is given and 24 hours pass, entry is permitted."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ze  si  lo-si-de ; bun mol  nu  re-ti  ki},  no-wi-fe-ka  [la-ze  ki  lo-ko-pa]`

**Written:** `go {laze si loside ; bun mol nu reti ki}, nowifeka [laze ki lokopa]`

**Gloss:** because [other-particular-person signals record ; twenty-four quantity-of-hour moves] [not-rule-forbidden] [that-person enters enclosed-space]

**Natural reading:** Once the party gives recorded notice and twenty-four hours pass, entry into the room is permitted.

**Notes:** `bun mol nu re-ti` gives the literal legal duration: twenty-four hours. The batch treats elapsed time as its own clause (`bun mol nu re-ti ki`) rather than hiding it inside a stacked prepositional phrase. That is the point of the test: Tonesu legal prose prefers discrete conditions over compressed English clause-bundles. `no-wi-fe-ka` confirms that permission is modeled as absence of prohibition, exactly as PERM-001 established.

---

### S924 — LSE-001-C

**Source:** "The contract permits entry for inspection or repair."

**Tonesu:** `la-ne-to-fe  ne  no-wi-fe-ka  [la-ze  ki  lo-ko-pa  wi {ka-se  lo-ko-pa / ka-de-be  lo-ko-pa}]`

**Written:** `lanetofe ne nowifeka [laze ki lokopa wi {kase lokopa / kadebe lokopa}]`

**Gloss:** [contract] IS [not-rule-forbidden] [other-particular-person enters enclosed-space in-order-that {inspects enclosed-space / repairs enclosed-space}]

**Natural reading:** The contract permits a party to enter the room in order to inspect or repair it.

**Notes:** `ne-to-fe` (W165) is extended from covenant/treaty into ordinary contract register: a formally bounded relation with stated terms. The purpose frame `wi {ka-se ... / ka-de-be ...}` supplies the limited purposes instead of relying on lexical implication. This is where the clause becomes recognizably contractual rather than merely deontic: the contract does not grant general presence, but bounded permission tied to inspection or repair.

## LSE-001 Batch Summary

**Entries:** S922–S924 · **New vocabulary:** none

| Entry | Tonesu | Written form | Key feature |
|-------|--------|-------------|-------------|
| S922 (LSE-001-A) | `go {la-ze no si lo-si-de}, wi-fe-ka [la-ze ki lo-ko-pa]` | `go {laze no si loside}, wifeka [laze ki lokopa]` | No recorded notice → entry forbidden |
| S923 (LSE-001-B) | `go {la-ze si lo-si-de ; bun mol nu re-ti ki}, no-wi-fe-ka [la-ze ki lo-ko-pa]` | `go {laze si loside ; bun mol nu reti ki}, nowifeka [laze ki lokopa]` | Exact 24-hour wait before permission |

**Key structural findings:**

**Finding 1: lease clauses decompose cleanly into deontic state + elapsed-time condition.** English compresses "without 24 hours' notice" into one surface phrase. Tonesu makes the same legal content clearer by splitting no-notice prohibition (S922) from waited-duration permission (S923).

**Finding 2: `si-de` is the right notice form for formal lease register.** The legal notice is not just a spoken signal; it is a signal that has entered the record. `si-de` therefore fits the contract domain better than bare `si`.

**Finding 3: exact legal duration works without special time vocabulary.** `bun mol nu re-ti` gives a literal twenty-four-hour wait in ordinary numeral grammar. No dedicated legal time unit or shorthand is required.

**Finding 4: contract purpose should be explicit.** S924 shows that the legal limit is not merely entry-after-delay, but entry-for-bounded-purpose. The `wi {inspection / repair}` frame captures that directly.

## GDPR-001 — GDPR Article 6(1): Lawful Bases for Processing (S925–S931)

**Batch:** GDPR-001  
**Source:** GDPR Article 6(1), paraphrasing the six lawful bases for processing personal data. Tests whether Tonesu can render the list as a battery of permissions rather than a disguised ontological taxonomy, and whether the overlap among the categories remains visible.  
**Entries:** S925–S931 · **New vocabulary:** none (all compositional)

**Key tests:**
- `lo-ka-to-ki lo-to-si ne i-zo-li` — act of processing personal data as a repeated legal object
- `ne-vo-wi` / `ne-to-fe` / `wi-fe` / `wi-ra` / `~vo-ne` — the lawful-basis battery
- `no-wi-fe-ka` — lawful basis as permission, not obligation
- list-structure verdict on the overlap between the categories

**Central claim:** GDPR 6(1) is not a list of six different kinds of truth. It is a list of six different ways a rule may suspend prohibition over the same act. Tonesu keeps that permission-structure explicit and therefore also exposes the statute's fuzzy catch-all: "legitimate interests" is the only basis that arrives already carrying `~`.

---

### S925 — GDPR-001-A

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if the data subject has given consent."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-i-zo-li  ne  ne-vo-wi  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {laizoli ne nevowi lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [particular-person IS consent-relation to act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When the person consents to the processing of that person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** `ne-vo-wi` (consent) is treated as a relation attaching to the act, not merely an emotional state. That matters legally: the basis is not "the person approves in general" but "the person stands in a consent relation to this act." `no-wi-fe-ka` keeps the article's logic correct: consent does not compel processing; it suspends prohibition.

---

### S926 — GDPR-001-B

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for the performance of a contract."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ne-to-fe  ne  wi-fe  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {lanetofe ne wife lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [contract IS rule over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When a contract requires the processing of a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** `ne-to-fe` is the formally bounded agreement. The article's contract basis is rendered here as the contract holding rule over the processing act. This is more precise than saying the contract merely "mentions" the act: the contract is the normative source making the act permissible.

---

### S927 — GDPR-001-C

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-wi-fe-su  ne  wi-fe  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {lawifesu ne wife lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [rule-structure IS rule over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When a legal rule requires the processing of a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** This basis looks superficially close to S926, but the source of normativity is different. In S926 the bounded agreement (`ne-to-fe`) is load-bearing; here the institutional rule-system (`wi-fe-su`) is. GDPR's list thereby distinguishes private bounded agreement from public rule structure, even when the resulting permission looks identical at the surface.

---

### S928 — GDPR-001-D

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary in order to protect vital interests."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-i-zo-li  zo-ra  de},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {laizoli zora de}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [particular-person vitality decreases] [act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When a person's vitality is in danger, the processing of that person's personal data is permitted.

**Notes:** `zo-ra` (vitality / life-energy) keeps the article's emergency basis grounded in organism-level preservation rather than abstraction. GDPR's phrasing says "vital interests"; the batch translates that as threat to animating life-condition. This is the clearest basis in the article: unlike consent or legitimate interests, the justification is not category-disputable once the emergency is real.

---

### S929 — GDPR-001-E

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ka-li-su  ne  wi-ra  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {lakalisu ne wira lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [governance IS authority over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When governance has official authority over the processing of a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** This is the article's public-task / official-authority arm rendered through `wi-ra`, not bare `wi-fe`. The distinction matters: `wi-fe` marks rule/obligation; `wi-ra` marks authorized exercise of office. GDPR keeps those apart, and the batch preserves the split.

---

### S930 — GDPR-001-F

**Source:** "Processing is lawful if it is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party."

**Tonesu:** `go {la-ze  ne  ~vo-ne  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li},  lo-ka-to-ki  lo-to-si  ne  i-zo-li  no-wi-fe-ka`

**Written:** `go {laze ne ~vone lokatoki lotosi ne izoli}, lokatoki lotosi ne izoli nowifeka`

**Gloss:** because [other-particular-person IS ~rightful-standing over act-of-processing information-signal related-to particular-person] [that processing-act] [not-rule-forbidden]

**Natural reading:** When another party has `~rightful-interest` in processing a person's personal data, the processing is permitted.

**Notes:** This is the article's catch-all basis, and it is the only one that arrives already carrying `~`. `~vo-ne` marks the phrase as structurally vague in the source system, not merely uncertain to the speaker. "Legitimate interests" is not a clean legal kind in the way consent or contract are. It is a source-system approximation whose boundary is intentionally left soft.

---

### S931 — GDPR-001-G

**Source:** Tonesu structural verdict on Article 6(1)

**Tonesu:** `la-wi-fe  ne  no-su-to  /  ke,  la-wi-fe  no  to-su-ki  lo-to-fe  {ne-vo-wi ; ne-to-fe ; wi-fe ; wi-ra ; ~vo-ne}`

**Written:** `lawife ne nosuto / ke, lawife no tosuki lotofe {nevowi ; netofe ; wife ; wira ; ~vone}`

**Gloss:** [the rule] IS [not-self-evident-truth] / but [the rule] NOT comprehends [the boundary] {consent ; contract ; rule ; authority ; ~rightful-interest}

**Natural reading:** The rule is not a self-evident truth / but the rule has not comprehended the boundary among consent, contract, legal obligation, authority, and `~legitimate-interest`.

**Notes:** Article 6(1) is a workable legal battery, not a coherent taxonomy. The sentence mirrors the statute's actual condition: the list functions, but its members are not all of one kind and they overlap structurally. `~vo-ne` is the most obvious outlier, but the real issue is broader: the article gives multiple ways to suspend prohibition without making their boundary relations explicit.

| S931 (GDPR-001-G) | `la-wi-fe ne no-su-to / ke, la-wi-fe no to-su-ki lo-to-fe {ne-vo-wi ; ne-to-fe ; wi-fe ; wi-ra ; ~vo-ne}` | `lawife ne nosuto / ke, lawife no tosuki lotofe {nevowi ; netofe ; wife ; wira ; ~vone}` | Structural verdict on category overlap |

**Key structural findings:**

**Finding 1: Article 6(1) is a permission battery.** Every basis ends in the same result: `no-wi-fe-ka` over the same processing act. The list is therefore not a taxonomy of processing kinds, but a set of independent ways prohibition can be suspended.

**Finding 2: contract, rule, and authority are distinct norm-sources.** S926, S927, and S929 look superficially similar in English legal prose, but Tonesu distinguishes them by source: bounded agreement (`ne-to-fe`), rule structure (`wi-fe-su`), and official authority (`wi-ra`).

**Finding 3: consent is relational, not atmospheric.** `ne-vo-wi` attaches to the act. That keeps consent from drifting into a vague pro-attitude and preserves its legal role as act-specific authorization.

**Finding 4: "legitimate interests" is the article's built-in `~` term.** S930 is the only basis that is honestly rendered with structural approximation from the start. The catch-all works politically and administratively precisely because its boundary is soft.

**Finding 5: the article functions better than it classifies.** S931 gives the batch verdict: the list works as law, but it is not `su-to`. The categories overlap and the rule does not fully comprehend their boundary relations.
