# CGV-001 — 31 December 1600 Charter Packet

## Status

Selected packet. This is the current best authority-defining text for `CGV-001`.

This packet upgrades the authority side from the 1817 East India Company volume's abstracted charter summary to a direct printed charter body. The operative clauses below are now verified against the Internet Archive page images for printed pages 3, 4, 7, 8, 11, and 12 of the 1887 Madras Government Press reprint. That still leaves room for a closer diplomatic or Patent Roll witness later, but the packet is no longer dependent on OCR alone for wording-sensitive use.

## Why this packet exists

This is the first recovered public surface that directly does the authority-defining work needed for `CGV-001`. ## Operative clauses recovered so far

### 1. Incorporation as a body corporate and politic

Image-checked reading from printed page 3:

- `... they and every of them from henceforth be, and shall be one Body Corporate and Politick, in Deed and in Name, by the Name of The Governor and Company of Merchants of London, Trading into the East-Indies ...`

Image check note:

- verified on printed page 3 / Archive `page/n28.jpg`
- the page also confirms the immediate succession and legal-capacity language that follows the incorporation formula

Structural value:

- directly constitutes the company as a legal body rather than merely describing an enterprise
- gives `CGV-001` a clean corporate authority-establishing clause

### 2. Governance vested in a first governor and named committees, with continuing succession

Image-checked reading from printed pages 3-4:

- `... we do assign, nominate, constitute and make, the said Thomas Smith, Alderman of London, to be the First and present Governor of the said Company ... until another of the said Company shall in due Manner be chosen and sworn ...`
- `... we do assign, nominate, constitute and make, the said Paul Banning, Leonard Hollyday, John Moore ... Committees.`

Image check note:

- verified on printed pages 3-4 / Archive `page/n28.jpg` and `page/n29.jpg`
- page 4 confirms the deputy, oath, and annual July election machinery that gives the office continuing succession rather than one-off honorary naming

Structural value:

- shows the charter does not stop at incorporation but immediately specifies an internal governing frame
- makes the corporate authority visibly administrative and durable rather than purely honorary

### 3. Internal lawmaking and punishment power for company government

Image-checked reading from printed pages 7-8:

- `... to make, ordain and constitute such, and so many reasonable Laws, Constitutions, Orders and Ordinances ... for the good Government of the same Company, and of all Factors, Masters, Mariners and other Officers ...`
- `... shall and may lawfully impose ... such Pains, Punishments and Penalties, by Imprisonment of Body, or by Fines and Amerciaments ...`

Image check note:

- verified on printed pages 7-8 / Archive `page/n32.jpg` and `page/n33.jpg`
- page 8 confirms the limiting condition that such laws, imprisonments, fines, and amerciaments must be reasonable and not contrary or repugnant to the laws, statutes, or customs of the realm

Structural value:

- gives the company rule-making power over its own operating body
- links internal governance directly to punishment and enforcement
- helps distinguish corporate privilege from a looser merchant association

### 4. Exclusive trade privilege tied to company membership, assembly rule, and sealed licence

Image-checked reading from printed pages 11-12:

- `... they ... shall, for the said Term of Fifteen Years, only have, use and enjoy, the whole entire and only Trade and Traffick, and the whole entire and only Liberty, Use and Privilege of trading and trafficking ...`
- `... according to the Orders, Ordinances and Agreements ... present at any Court or Publick Assembly ... and not otherwise ...`
- `... unless it be by and with such Licence and Agreement of The said Governor and Company of Merchants of London, Trading into the East-Indies, in writing first had and obtained, under their Common Seal ...`

Image check note:

- verified on printed pages 11-12 / Archive `page/n36.jpg` and `page/n37.jpg`
- page 12 confirms that the exclusive-trade grant is enforced against outside subjects, with a sealed licence as the only stated exception

Structural value:

- makes clear that chartered commerce is exclusive and institutionally gated
- ties trade privilege to internal company order and sealed corporate licence rather than to atomized private merchants
- gives the authority side a direct bridge into the later governance-side territorial administration text
