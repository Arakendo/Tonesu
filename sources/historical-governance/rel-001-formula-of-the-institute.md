# REL-001 — Formula of the Institute Packet

## Status

Selected packet. This is the current best authority-defining text for `REL-001`.

This packet settles the authority side of the Jesuit specimen around the 1540 *Formula of the Institute* as preserved in the papal bull *Regimini militantis Ecclesiae*. The current working surface is the Boston College Jesuit Portal transcription and English translation. That makes it a materially stronger public surface than a generic secondary summary, though wording-sensitive arguments may still want a later Latin-side check against the cited critical edition.

## Source

- Title: `Formula of the Institute`
- Historical setting: inserted into Pope Paul III's bull `Regimini militantis Ecclesiae`
- Date: 1540
- Public surface: `https://jesuitportal.bc.edu/research/documents/1540_formula/`
- Host: Portal to Jesuit Studies, Institute for Advanced Jesuit Studies, Boston College
- Source note on page: the formula was composed by Ignatius and his first companions in 1539 and inserted with minor revisions into the 1540 bull; it was revised again in 1550 in `Exposcit Debitum`
- Original Latin source cited on page: `Pauli III Prima Institutionis Bulla et Confirmationis Societatis Iesu, MI Const I. Roma: Borgo S. Spirito, 5, 1934, pg. 24-32.`
- English translation cited on page: `The Constitutions of the Society of Jesus and Their Complementary Norms: A Complete English Translation of the Official Latin Texts` (Institute of Jesuit Sources, 1996), pp. 3-13

## Why this packet exists

`REL-001` needs an authority-defining text that shows what kind of body the Society of Jesus claims to be, what higher authority it binds itself to, how internal governance is nested, and where assignment and obedience first become institutionally visible. The *Formula of the Institute* does all of that in compact form.

It is therefore the cleanest starting point for the religious / moral authority control specimen: the Society's legitimacy is explicitly spiritual, but its routing structure is already administrative.

## Operative clauses located so far

### 1. The Society defines itself as service under Christ's banner and the Roman Pontiff

Boston College public text:

- `Whoever wishes to serve as a soldier of God beneath the banner of the cross in our Society, which we desire to be designated by the name of Jesus, and to serve the Lord alone and his vicar on earth ...`

Structural value:

- directly defines the order's authority claim in spiritual and institutional terms at once
- ties the Society's identity to a superior authority rather than to autonomous association

### 2. Distribution of employments and constitutive governance belong to the superior and council

Boston College public text:

- `The selection of each one's grade as well as the entire distribution of employments shall be in the power of the superior or prelate who is to be elected by us ...`
- `This superior, with the advice of his associates, shall possess ... the authority to establish constitutions ...`
- `All right to execute and command, however, will be in the power of the superior.`

Structural value:

- makes the order's authority visibly nested: superior, associates, council, whole Society
- shows that assignment and command are not merely exhortative but routed powers
- gives `REL-001` an explicit internal-governance clause rather than only a spirituality text

### 3. The Society binds itself by a special vow of mission obedience to the pope

Boston College public text:

- `... we have judged that it is of the greatest profit to us to go beyond the ordinary obligations and bind ourselves by a special vow, so that whatever the present Roman Pontiff and others to come will wish to command us with regard to the progress of souls and the propagation of the faith ... we will obey at once, without subterfuge or excuse ...`
- `... wherever he may be pleased to send us to any regions whatsoever ...`

Structural value:

- makes mission-routing obedience a constitutive feature, not a later custom
- sharply pressures the distinction between spiritual legitimacy and administratively organized assignment

### 4. Members are forbidden to negotiate their own missions and remain under routed assignment

Boston College public text:

- `... to forestall among us any ambition for such missions or provinces, or any refusal of them, let each one promise never to carry on negotiations with the Roman pontiff about such missions directly or indirectly, but to leave all this care to God and to his vicar and to the superior of the Society.`

Structural value:

- assignment is neither self-directed nor freely bargained
- clarifies that mission placement is routed through hierarchy rather than individual charisma or preference

### 5. Observance of the rule requires obedience to the superior, who issues commands toward the Institute's end

Boston College public text:

- `All should likewise vow that in all matters that concern the observance of this Rule they will obey the superior of the Society.`
- `The superior, however, should issue the commands which he knows to be opportune for achieving the end set before him by God and by the Society.`

Structural value:

- gives `REL-001` a direct rule-obedience clause internal to the order
- links command to institutional purpose rather than to private preference
